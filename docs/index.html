Features

Everything a control room needs.
Nothing it doesn't.

Real-time AI detection

A vision model scans every frame of every camera and flags what you told it to watch — phones out of the box — within about a second, even small, half-hidden ones.

Photo evidence, automatically

Every detection is saved with the photo, timestamp, camera and confidence. Confirm or dismiss each one to keep a clean, reviewable log.

Any camera works

Your laptop’s webcam, any phone’s browser via a link — no app to install — or real rtsp:// CCTV. Mix and match as many as you like.

Display wall

A fullscreen monitoring wall built for a big screen — with an unmissable on-screen alarm, flash and beep when something is found.

Alerts on your phone

Optional Telegram alerts send the photo and location straight to invigilators walking the room.

Roles for your team

Admins manage cameras and settings; invigilators watch feeds and handle alerts. Everyone sees exactly what they need.

How it works

Watching in three steps.

Add a camera

Press “+ Add camera”, give it a name and the location it watches. A built-in guide walks first-timers through every option.

Aim it & test

Point it where people sit, then hold a phone up in front of it. Within a second a red alert with a photo appears — that's Vigil working.

Let it watch

Vigil monitors every feed all the time, viewed or not. Rearrange your camera wall by simply dragging the panels.

Setup

Any camera you already own.
Ready in about 2 minutes.

This computer's webcam

The simplest start — one click, no URL needed. Perfect for trying Vigil out on your desk right now.

Any phone — just a link

Add a camera, choose “A device’s camera”, and open the link it gives you on the phone. Allow the camera — that’s it. No app to install.

CCTV / IP cameras

Paste the camera's rtsp://… stream URL and it joins the wall like any other feed.

Where do the photos go? Every detection photo is stored on the computer running Vigil — in its local evidence folder and Evidence Log. Nothing is uploaded to any server, and only alerts you enable (like Telegram) ever leave the machine.
For universities & institutions

Run a pilot yourselves.
One computer, up to 10 cameras.

No procurement, no integration project, no vendor on site. Your exam team can have Vigil watching a real hall in under an hour — and everything it records stays on your own machine.

Install on one computer

Any modern Mac or Windows PC in the exam hall or control room. Download, double-click, create the admin login. That machine holds all footage and evidence — nothing leaves it.

Point it at your cameras

Paste rtsp:// links from your existing CCTV/NVR, use spare phones or laptops as cameras via a link, and tag each one with its location — “Hall B · Row 5”. Up to 10 cameras is a comfortable pilot on one machine.

Run a mock exam & review

Invigilators watch the Live Monitor or the fullscreen Display wall; each detection needs a human Confirm — AI flags, people decide. Afterwards, the Evidence Log shows every incident with photo, time, camera and reviewer decision.

What to check at the end of the pilot: detections caught vs. missed, false alarms per exam, and whether invigilators found alerts faster than walking the aisles. If the numbers convince you, scale up — Vigil has no per-camera fee.
Download

Get Vigil on your computer.

Free, no account. On Mac it installs like any app — drag it into Applications and open it. The first launch sets up its AI components once (~2 GB); every launch after that is instant.

Download for Mac Windows & Linux (.zip)

Mac — drag & drop

Open the downloaded Vigil.dmg and drag Vigil into your Applications folder — just like any Mac app. Double-click to open. First time only: right-click → Open → Open (macOS checks new apps once). Your browser opens to Vigil — create an admin account and you're watching.

Windows

Unzip the download and double-click Vigil-Windows.bat. If SmartScreen warns, choose More info → Run anyway. If it asks for Python, install it with "Add Python to PATH" ticked, then run it again.

Linux

Unzip and run ./Vigil-Linux.sh in a terminal — it sets itself up on the first run and opens the dashboard when ready.
